## TL;DR

Software development has not fundamentally changed in the age of AI  despite how it feels, what has changed is how we work. Writing code is taking up less of our time, this does not change our core value of producing reliable, robust systems that solve real problems.

## Why it matters

Change is coming regardless of whether we want it or not, a new way of working will either be thrust upon us or we can design it from the ground up. Thinking about this now gives us the opportunity to shape how we work rather than being the victims of poorly designed workflows.

## Go Deeper

Adapting to changes in software development lately has been challenging. Lots of change, tools updating, new business expectations, chaotic decisions flowing, AI entering key areas, and our core skill turned from write to edit. Navigating the new era of software development as AI workflows begin to settle takes work. The new ways of working are more about adaptation than it is about radical change. We are more fractured amongst tasks with more code to keep to a high standard. It is not all grim as our core value has not changed, there is still plenty to learn, and we are finally seeing some convergence on workflows.

### The workflow has not changed the emphasis has

There is a large amount of new workflows, different tricks, endless tips, weird hacks, and 'perfect' skills going around. The hype train makes this sound new and glamorous but the new workflows are just a change of emphasis rather than a complete overhaul. The base workflow before was ticket, plan, write/test, review, merge, with most of the time spent on write. Now we get ticket, plan, write/test/generate, review, merge. The change is from most of the time being spent on write to being spent on test, plan and reviews.

### Multitasking is a hidden cost

Does running multiple AI windows/tabs/panes count as multitasking? Does the loss of deep focus affect the quality of output? With the added expectations of speed, will this affect devs? Hours of hard focus have been replaced by fractured attention and more expectations. Multitasking research has shown switching between tasks to introduce measurable costs including slower responses, and reduced accuracy (Hinss et al., 2024). Deep in the weeds blocks of time is where we used to learn nuance of the system and the relationship it has with the domain. The underlying promise of increased output by the AI hype train and the poor ways to measure risk producing poorer work environments for tech teams.

### More code means more quality gates

The speed at which AI can produce artefacts outruns how much our minds can hold at once. Code generation is so fast and so much that relying on human review is not rigorous enough, we need specs and gates, preferably executable ones. Specs give us a standard that the AI needs to live up to, as long as it is not editable by the implementation agent itself. A spec like this captures intent and the meeting of expectations. Review gates on the other side are a stamp of approval. Use tools like linters, compilers, automated tests and review bots. This will be the new focus of an engineer as code production gets further away from us.

### Domain knowledge is becoming even more valuable

Learning a new language, the nuances of a framework or the ins and outs of a specific API are no longer the only game as a dev. In contrast it is architecture, domains, pipelines, system design, testing, quality assurance, and soft skills that are becoming a key piece in the game of engineering. Up-skilling beyond code is where the value is. The elite programmer used to get all of the kudos, they would know the system inside out, and was looked upon like a miracle worker. They never had time to do the other work, now we do. With time the dev that knows the business, and understands cross department issues has the baton of excellence. Defining the issue or problem completely is where the miracles happen in a time of cheap code artefacts.

### Know the value you create

Do you know what value you are bringing to the table? Does your boss know? Is it muddy and hard to pinpoint at the moment? Keeping proof of the value you provide in this time of turbulence is worth it. Know your own value by recording it, just because AI wrote most of the code does not mean you gave away ownership. Keeping a record of value allows you to refresh yourself going into quarterly reviews, in this storm of multitasking it is easy to forget. If you can't say what you are bringing to the table, no one else will be able to.

Our core skills are not devalued. I would argue that they are more valuable today. Problem solving and systems still have huge value in today's market. Systems that were too costly to build are now becoming viable. Problems in new areas are coming to a head, old problems still exist, the corporate machine keeps chugging and it will always need fixing, maintaining and new inventions.

## Final thoughts

The work and adjustment ahead has just begun. The new workflows emphasise different areas of the software development life cycle but they don't change our fundamental output. Managing a fractured, multitasking mind is a new monster in the room, but it has been slain before. The amount of output can get overwhelming, build calm confidence into it by using specs and review gates. Continue to up-skill around code, those adjacent skills matter more than ever. Keep proof of value for yourself as a foundation to anchor on. Your core business value has not changed, solve problems by building systems. As workflows converge, what does your day to day look like? Are you designing your new way of working or letting it happen around you?
